A £50m budget is really a question of where a marginal pound buys the most points. Three seasons of your league's own drafts, joined to what each player actually scored, answer it. The short version: attack is a cliff, defence is a plateau.

Paying up works in attack. In defence, the curve is flat — and the market knows it.

A £15m+ forward returned 159 points; a cheap one just 54 — spending buys real points. But defenders top out around 104 however much you pay, and your league has never once paid £15m for one. The whole top-to-bottom defender upgrade is worth ~30 points.

The trade-off, settled: an extra £3m upgrading a decent defender buys ~20–30 points; the same £3m lifting a second striker off the 43-point replacement floor buys 50–75. The pound belongs in attack. The one edge the field misses: top defenders project 160+ yet sell for £2–10m — grab them cheap, never in a bidding war.
